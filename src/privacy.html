3. The three ways a message can be sent

Every chat uses one delivery mode. The strictest preference of the people in a direct chat wins. You can see the mode in the app.

  • Standard. Sent over an encrypted connection (TLS) and stored on our servers as readable text, so everyone in the chat sees the history and so can the operator. Link previews are made by our server, which fetches the linked page. Moderators can see a Standard message that is reported.
  • Ephemeral. End-to-end encrypted with MLS between the devices in the chat. Our servers relay encrypted data and can see who it is for and when, but not what it says. A message waits on our server only until your device confirms it arrived, and never longer than 7 days. The readable history lives only in an encrypted database on your own devices. Deleting a message and disappearing-message timers for these chats are being built. [OWNER: until they ship, say that messages cannot be deleted after sending; once they ship, describe them here and in section 6] Our servers cannot fetch link previews for them.
  • Peer-to-peer. Sent straight between the devices of the people in the chat, with our servers only passing connection set-up data. Nothing is queued or stored on a server: if the other person is offline the message is not delivered. While you are connected, your devices may see each other's IP addresses.

Voice and video calls can also be end-to-end encrypted (DAVE). Private modes never fall back to Standard: if a private message cannot be sent securely, it is not sent.

Because ephemeral and peer-to-peer content is not on our servers, we cannot recover it, produce it on request or review it. A report from such a chat only contains what the reporter submits.

4. Notifications

On iPhone, notifications are delivered through Apple's push notification service (APNs). Apple receives the push token, and the notification content passes through Apple's servers. For a Standard chat the notification can contain the sender's name, the server and channel, and a preview of the text; you can switch previews off per device in the app. For ephemeral and peer-to-peer chats the notification never contains message text, only a generic alert. Incoming-call alerts use Apple's PushKit and include the caller's name only for Standard chats. Notifications for Android are not sent yet.

7. Your choices and rights

In the app you can edit your profile, change your email and password, turn on two-factor authentication, see and sign out your devices, block people, report content, choose a delivery mode and delete your account. Under data protection law you may also ask us for access to your data, correction, deletion, restriction, a portable copy, or object to processing, and you may complain to the supervisory authority. In the Netherlands that is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. It asks you to contact us first.

There is no self-service data export in the app yet. Send your request to {{contactEmail}}; we will answer within one month (GDPR Art. 12(3)); for complex requests we may extend this by up to two further months and will tell you why. We cannot export or delete ephemeral and peer-to-peer messages from our side, because they only exist on the devices of the people in the chat.

9. Security

Connections use TLS. Passwords and refresh tokens are stored hashed. On iPhone the session is kept in the system Keychain and the encrypted message database stays on the device, excluded from device backups. Our admin tools are behind an IP restriction and Cloudflare Access, and every administrative action is written to an audit log. No system is perfectly secure, and Standard messages are readable by the operator by design.
